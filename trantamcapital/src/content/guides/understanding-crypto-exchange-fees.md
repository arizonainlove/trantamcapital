Exchange fees directly impact your trading profitability. Understanding the fee structures of different platforms can save you significant money over time.

## Trading Fee Structures

Most major exchanges use a tiered fee model based on your **30-day trading volume**. Higher volume traders pay lower fees. **Binance** offers some of the most competitive fees in the industry, especially when using their native BNB token for fee discounts.

| Fee Component | Typical Range | Notes |
|--------------|--------------|-------|
| Spot Trading Fee | **0.02% – 0.10%** | Lower with volume tiers |
| Futures Trading Fee | **0.01% – 0.05%** | Maker/taker model |
| Withdrawal Fee | **Fixed per crypto** | Varies by network congestion |

## Maker vs Taker Fees

Understanding the maker/taker model is essential. **Makers** add liquidity to the order book (limit orders) and pay lower fees. **Takers** remove liquidity (market orders) and pay higher fees. On platforms like **Bybit** and **OKX**, this distinction can significantly affect your trading costs.

## Hidden Costs to Watch For

Beyond trading fees, watch for:

- **Spread costs** — The difference between buy and sell prices
- **Deposit fees** — Some exchanges charge for fiat deposits
- **Inactivity fees** — Less common but worth checking
- **Network fees** — Blockchain transaction costs for withdrawals

## Conclusion

Fee comparison should be a key part of your exchange selection process. Even small differences in fee structures compound significantly for active traders.

*Disclaimer: Fee structures change frequently. Always verify current rates on the exchange's official website.*
