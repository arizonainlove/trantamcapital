Margin trading allows you to borrow funds to increase your trading position size. While it can amplify profits, it equally amplifies losses and requires careful risk management.

## How Margin Trading Works

When you open a margin trade, you provide a portion of the capital (margin) and borrow the rest from the exchange. For example, with **2x leverage**, you can control **$2,000** worth of crypto with only **$1,000** of your own funds.

Exchanges like **Binance**, **Bybit**, and **OKX** offer margin trading with varying leverage levels and borrowing terms.

## Key Concepts to Understand

| Concept | Definition |
|---------|-----------|
| **Margin** | Your own capital used as collateral |
| **Leverage** | Multiplier on your position size |
| **Liquidation Price** | Price at which your position is closed |
| **Maintenance Margin** | Minimum equity required to keep position open |

## Liquidation Risk

The biggest danger in margin trading is liquidation. If the market moves against your position and your equity falls below the maintenance margin requirement, the exchange will close your position — locking in your loss.

## Risk Management Strategies

- Use **stop-loss orders** to limit potential losses
- Avoid using maximum leverage — **3x to 5x** is more manageable for beginners
- Start with isolated margin to limit risk to specific positions
- Never margin trade with funds you cannot afford to lose

## Margin Trading vs Futures Trading

Margin trading involves borrowing actual funds to buy or sell assets, while futures trading uses derivative contracts. Both involve leverage but operate differently in terms of settlement and contract structure.

## Conclusion

Margin trading is a powerful tool but demands respect. Educate yourself thoroughly, start with small positions, and always prioritize capital preservation.

*Disclaimer: Margin trading carries significant risk of rapid financial loss. You can lose more than your initial deposit. Trade with extreme caution.*
